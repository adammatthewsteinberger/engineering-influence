A book's config can now say `kind = "novel"` (the default is `nonfiction`), and the kind picks the text rules. A novel may use em dashes, in the manuscript and in the checks of the finished PDF and EPUB, where non-fiction still refuses them; en dashes are refused for both. A `---` line inside a novel's chapter prints a scene break instead of nothing: a centred `* * *` in the PDF and the hardcover, a classed paragraph in the EPUB, with the paragraph after it not indented; a rule that opens or ends a passage, sits beside another rule or is inside a quote or list prints nothing, and a rule right after a callout or illustration is kept. The word `Draft` is still refused in a novel's narration but may appear inside curly quotation marks, so a character can say it; a quoted span is capped at 500 characters so an unclosed quote cannot hide a stray `Draft`. An unknown kind stops the build with a message naming `nonfiction` and `novel`. The rules live in a small `TextPolicy` model that the loader, both document builders and both verifiers share, and `BookConfigV1` gains an optional `kind`. Non-fiction output is unchanged except that every EPUB's stylesheet gains two rules for scene breaks. A synthetic novel (`tests/fixtures/fixture_novel/`) joins the non-fiction fixture in the toolchain test, which now builds both with the real toolchain and states what the novel must show.
